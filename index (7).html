The game plan

Your posting names four jobs, plus two that run alongside them. Here is how I would take them on, and what my first 90 days look like.

Capability strategy

Find the capabilities that drive results and turn business goals into development plans.

Leader and manager development

Programs at every level, with assessments, 360-degree feedback, and coaching.

Onboarding and role readiness

Pre-boarding through day 90, owned from start to finish.

Show what worked, tie it to business results, and adjust.

Alongside these: supporting the VP on the annual goal-setting and performance management cycle, and managing and growing the Learning Coordinator. You can try both on the Leaders slide.

Listen and get a baseline

  • Meet the VP, business leaders, and HR business partners. Ask each: what should your people do better by next season?
  • Work a game-night shift alongside a team to see the real job.
  • Audit what exists: onboarding steps, the Workday Learning catalog, and the performance cycle calendar.
  • Set up weekly 1:1s and a shared tracker with the Learning Coordinator.
  • Pull baseline numbers: time-to-proficiency, course completion, and engagement themes.

Don't build anything big yet.

Design and pilot

  • Map the capabilities behind the top three business goals.
  • Draft the onboarding blueprint (pre-boarding, day one, first 90 days) and pilot it with one team.
  • Build the manager kit: first-week checklist, 1:1 agenda, and feedback scripts.
  • Work with the VP on the goal-setting toolkit and the calendar for the next cycle.
  • Publish the first two skill pathways as drafts and ask managers to mark them up.

Pilot small, learn fast.

Launch and prove it

  • Roll the onboarding path out to every new hire.
  • Run the first leader cohort with an assessment and a 360 debrief.
  • Put the pathways into Workday Learning so people can find them.
  • Give the VP a first readout: what we collected, what it says, and what changes next.

Show the data, then adjust.

Quarter 1

From business goal to development plan

Pick a goal. Reveal each link in the chain, one at a time. I start with the result the business wants and work backward to the smallest learning that gets it.

Choose a goal to start the play.

Capability gap check: is it a training problem?

Before I build anything, I test whether training can fix the gap. Decide for each signal. Wrong answers explain why.

Where training is the answer, I put it in the flow of work: a three-minute video at the gate, a job aid at the desk, not a day away from the floor.

The method

Action mapping: begin with the business result, name the behaviors that would move it, find what stops people from doing them, then build only what removes that barrier. Sometimes the answer is a job aid, a better tool, or a clearer goal rather than a course.
